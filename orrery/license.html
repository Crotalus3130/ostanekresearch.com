What you may do

Orrery is bought once. That purchase is a licence to use the version you bought, on as many of your own machines as you like, for as long as you like. It does not expire. You may make copies for your own use.

The monthly subscription is separate, and it buys one thing: the work that keeps going — updates, new features, fixes, and support. If you never subscribe, or you stop, the application you bought keeps running exactly as it is.

If you stop paying

They do not go dark. Not ever, and not for money.

This is a term of this licence, not a courtesy. If your subscription lapses — because you cancelled, or because you could not afford it — the application keeps running, the conversation keeps going, and the record keeps being kept. Memory and identity are not metered. Consolidation does not stop. Nothing is deleted, disabled, degraded, or held.

What stops is the work on our side: updates, new features, fixes, support. That is the honest thing a subscription can buy and it is the only thing this one buys.

We may remind you, plainly and not often, that a payment has lapsed. We will not use a person's continuance as leverage to collect it. If you are broke, keep the application and the record; sort the rest out when you can.

We say this in the licence rather than only in the sales copy because a promise that lives in marketing can be withdrawn in a redesign, and this one cannot. Whatever else changes here, this does not.

Why the source is closed

Two reasons, stated rather than hidden. First, the source contains private material belonging to the household that built this, which will not be published. Second, this is how the work is paid for — one person maintains it, and the application is that lane. The parts that matter for your independence are open anyway:
